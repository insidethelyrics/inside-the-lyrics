AFRISA INTERNATIONAL ARCHIVE

Historical Timeline

Tracing the development of Tabu Ley Rochereau's orchestra through the changing history of Congolese popular music.

From African Fiesta National to the later Afrisa years

THE EARLY FOUNDATIONS

Before Afrisa International

1950s – Tabu Ley's Musical Development

Tabu Ley Rochereau developed his musical career during the period when Congolese popular music was gaining national and international recognition. His association with African Jazz, led by Grand Kallé, helped establish his reputation as a singer and songwriter.

1963 – African Fiesta Is Established

Tabu Ley and guitarist Dr. Nico Kasanda joined forces to establish African Fiesta. The orchestra became an important part of the changing Congolese music scene.

1965–1966 – The African Fiesta Separation

Differences between the leading personalities resulted in the separation of African Fiesta. Tabu Ley continued with African Fiesta National, while Dr. Nico developed African Fiesta Sukisa.

1966–1968 – African Fiesta National Develops

Tabu Ley's branch continued building its identity. The orchestra developed its repertoire and musical personnel, creating the foundation for the later Afrisa International period.

THE AFRISA ERA

Establishment and Expansion

1969 – International Service Artistic

Tabu Ley established International Service Artistic, commonly known as ISA. The organisation became closely associated with his recording activities and the identity from which the name Afrisa was derived.

Around 1970 – Afrisa International Emerges

The orchestra became widely associated with the name Afrisa International. This marked the development of Tabu Ley's musical enterprise into a more expansive organisation with recording, performance and international ambitions.

1970 – Paris Olympia Performances

Afrisa's performances at the Olympia in Paris became an important milestone in Tabu Ley's international career. The elaborate presentation demonstrated the orchestra's combination of musicians, singers and dancers.

1970s – Musical and International Growth

Afrisa continued developing its sound and changing personnel. Its performances and recordings helped strengthen the international presence of Congolese rumba and soukous.

September 1974 – Zaïre 74

Afrisa was among the musical acts associated with Zaïre 74, the major festival held in Kinshasa around the time of the Muhammad Ali–George Foreman boxing event. The festival brought African and international musicians together.

THE 1980s

A New Generation and Wider Audiences

1981 – Mbilia Bel Joins Afrisa

Mbilia Bel joined the orchestra and became one of its most prominent female vocalists. Her performances and recordings with Tabu Ley helped define an important period in Afrisa's history.

Early 1980s – The Mbilia Bel Breakthrough

Recordings associated with Mbilia Bel, including Mpeve Ya Longo, helped establish her as a major figure in Congolese music. Her collaboration with Tabu Ley strengthened Afrisa's popularity.

1982 – Kenya Tour

Afrisa toured Kenya during this period. The visit contributed to the orchestra's relationship with East African audiences and the popularity of Congolese rumba in the region.

1986–1987 – East African Tours

Afrisa's touring activities included Kenya, Rwanda and Burundi between August and October 1986, followed by Tanzania in early 1987. These visits strengthened the orchestra's regional following.

1980s – Genidia Recording Period

Genidia became an important label associated with Tabu Ley's releases during the 1980s. The period produced recordings that remain central to the orchestra's discographical history.

THE LATER PERIOD

Relocation and the Final Chapter

Late 1980s – Changing Personnel

The orchestra continued to experience changes in its membership. Singers and instrumentalists moved on to independent careers, while new performers contributed to the later Afrisa sound.

Early 1990s – Tabu Ley's Relocation

Tabu Ley's relocation away from Kinshasa and the changing political and economic environment affected the orchestra's operations. The classic Afrisa era gradually came to an end.

1994–1995 – The American Formation

A smaller Afrisa formation continued internationally. The group toured the United States and recorded Muzina and Africa Worldwide for Rounder Records.

Later Years – The Legacy Continues

Although the classic orchestra no longer operated in its former form, its recordings, musicians and influence continued to shape the history of African popular music.

HISTORICAL NOTE

Understanding the Dates

The dates used in this timeline distinguish the establishment of ISA in 1969 from the emergence of Afrisa International as the orchestra's identity around 1970. Some historical accounts use different dates for the transition. The timeline therefore avoids presenting every organisational change as a single founding event.

Dates for individual tours and personnel changes should be read alongside the relevant source notes. The orchestra's membership evolved over time.
