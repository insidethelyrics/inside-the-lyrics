Orchestra Vévé: Historical Timeline

From Verckys's beginnings to a lasting musical legacy

Follow the life and musical development of Verckys Kiamuangana Mateta, the formation of Orchestra Vévé and the growth of one of the important music enterprises in Congolese popular music.

Chapter One · Before Orchestra Vévé

1944–1968: The Early Years of Verckys

19 May 1944 — Birth of Verckys

Georges Kiamuangana Mateta, later known as Verckys, was born on 19 May 1944 in Kisantu, in the Belgian Congo.

Early 1960s — Musical development

Verckys developed his musical skills through participation in different Kinshasa musical formations. Biographical accounts associate him with groups including Los Cantina, Jamel National, Congo Jazz, Oui Fifi and Conga Succès.

These experiences helped establish his reputation as a saxophonist before he became widely associated with OK Jazz.

1963 — Association with OK Jazz

Verckys joined OK Jazz, the orchestra led by Franco Luambo. He became associated with the group's instrumental sound and recording activities.

His time in OK Jazz provided important professional experience before he established his own musical formation.

1968 — Preparing for independence

By the end of the 1960s, Verckys had established himself as an experienced instrumentalist. His eventual departure from OK Jazz opened the way for an independent musical project.

Chapter Two · A new orchestra

1969: The Formation of Orchestra Vévé

1969 — Departure from OK Jazz

Verckys left OK Jazz following professional tensions involving musical leadership and recording activities.

The circumstances of his departure are described differently in historical accounts, so the disagreement should not be reduced to a single unqualified explanation.

1969 — Establishment of Orchestra Vévé

Verckys established Orchestra Vévé in Kinshasa. The orchestra became the centre of his independent performing career and a platform for developing new singers and instrumentalists.

One biographical account attributes the founding to 5 April 1969. The year 1969 is the safer established date used throughout this archive.

1969 onwards — Early vocal identity

Singers associated with the early formation included Matadidi Mario, Djeskain, Saak-Sakul and Bovic Bondo. Their performances helped shape the orchestra's early identity.

Chapter Three · Expansion and popularity

1970–1973: Growth and Musical Recognition

Early 1970s — Development of the Vévé sound

Orchestra Vévé continued developing its identity through Congolese rumba, dance rhythms, electric guitar arrangements and prominent instrumental passages.

Verckys's saxophone remained an important element of its musical character.

Around 1970 — Growth of the Vévé enterprise

Verckys increasingly developed activities beyond live performance. His wider music enterprise became associated with Éditions Vévé, recording, production and artist development.

Historical accounts differ on the precise date of the label's establishment. Its development should therefore be understood as a process rather than assigned one uncontested founding date.

1972 — Recording studio development

Accounts identify the establishment of an important modern recording studio in Kinshasa in 1972. This investment strengthened Verckys's position as a music entrepreneur.

Around 1972 — Trio Madjesi emerges

The singers Matadidi Mario, Djeskain and Sinatra became associated with the name Trio Madjesi.

Their popularity and independent musical direction became a significant development in the history of Orchestra Vévé. They later became associated with Orchestra Sosoliso.

The exact chronology of their separation and subsequent activities requires further discographical verification.

1972 — Nakomitunaka

Verckys's composition Nakomitunaka became an important part of his solo recording legacy.

The song is notable for engaging with questions of religious authority and the cultural environment of Mobutu's authenticity campaign.

Chapter Four · East African connection

1974: Orchestra Vévé in Kenya

1974 — A reported two-month tour

Orchestra Vévé undertook a reported two-month tour of Kenya. This visit forms an important part of the orchestra's international history.

1974 — Nairobi recordings

Recordings associated with the Kenyan period include:

  • Bassala Hot
  • Cheka Sana
  • Talali Talala

These recordings are documented in the historical notes accompanying the Analog Africa compilation Verckys et l'Orchestre Vévé.

The Kenya connection illustrates the growing movement of Congolese musicians and recordings into East African markets.

Chapter Five · The wider enterprise

The 1970s: Éditions Vévé and Expansion

1970s — Expansion of recording and production

Verckys continued developing recording, production and artist promotion activities through the wider Éditions Vévé enterprise.

The enterprise became connected with recordings by numerous Congolese musicians and orchestras. It should not be confused with Orchestra Vévé's own membership.

1978 — Vévé Centre

The opening of Vévé Centre in 1978 represented another stage in Verckys's development as an entertainment entrepreneur.

His activities demonstrated how Congolese musicians could develop businesses that extended beyond recording and performing.

Chapter Six · Changing responsibilities

1980s–2000s: Later Years and Preservation

1980s — Changing musical priorities

By the 1980s, Verckys's interests increasingly extended into music production, business and industry leadership.

Orchestra Vévé's original personnel and musical identity had changed over time as musicians pursued other opportunities.

The available evidence does not establish one definitive date for the orchestra's formal dissolution.

Later years — Music-industry leadership

Verckys became associated with leadership in musicians' organisations, including the Union of Zairian Musicians and the later Union of Congolese Musicians.

2006 — Éditions Vévé revival

Historical accounts describe renewed activity around Éditions Vévé in 2006, including the reissue and preservation of recordings from its catalogue.

Chapter Seven · Remembering Verckys

2022: The Passing of Verckys

13 October 2022 — Verckys dies

Verckys Kiamuangana Mateta died in Kinshasa on 13 October 2022, aged 78.

His passing marked the end of the life of a musician whose influence extended across performance, recording, production and music-industry leadership.

19 December 2022 — Burial

Reports identify 19 December 2022 as the date of his burial at Nécropole de la N'sele in Kinshasa.

A continuing musical legacy

Orchestra Vévé's recordings, the story of Trio Madjesi and the wider Éditions Vévé catalogue continue to provide important material for understanding Congolese popular music.

Chronological and Editorial Notes

  • 1969 is the established formation year of Orchestra Vévé. The exact founding day of 5 April is attributed to one biographical account.
  • The date of the establishment of Éditions Vévé varies across historical accounts.
  • The early 1970s Trio Madjesi chronology should not be treated as a fully verified exact sequence of departures and recordings.
  • The Kenya tour and Nairobi recording details are based on retrospective release notes.
  • The orchestra's later decline should not be confused with a documented formal dissolution date.
