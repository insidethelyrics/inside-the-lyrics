RECORDING HISTORY

Understanding Afrisa's Discography

Afrisa International's recording history reflects the changing music industry in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. During the earlier period, many songs appeared on 45 rpm singles. Some were later collected into albums for local and international markets.

As a result, an album title found on a later European or American release does not necessarily represent the original date on which every song was recorded or first issued.

This archive distinguishes selected recordings, live performances, original releases and later compilations wherever the available evidence allows.

RECORD LABELS

ISA and Genidia

International Service Artistic (ISA)

ISA was established by Tabu Ley in 1969 and became closely connected with his recording activities. The organisation's name contributed to the identity Afrisa International.

ISA represents an important part of Tabu Ley's effort to develop a recording enterprise around his musical work.

Genidia

Genidia became an important label associated with Tabu Ley and Afrisa during the 1980s. It is particularly relevant when tracing the orchestra's later album releases.

International Reissues

Afrisa recordings have appeared through various international distributors and retrospective collections. These releases have helped preserve the music for new audiences, but their publication dates should not be confused with original recording dates.

SELECTED REPERTOIRE

Important Songs and Recordings

Tabu Ley and Afrisa Repertoire

  • Africa Mokili Mobimba
  • Lisanga Ya Bambanda
  • Nairobi
  • Kaful Mayayi

These titles provide starting points for exploring the orchestra's wider repertoire. Individual versions, recording dates and original release details should be matched to their specific record issues.

Mbilia Bel and Afrisa

  • Mpeve Ya Longo
  • Eswi Yo Wapi
  • Boya Ye
  • Faux Pas
  • Quelle Méchanceté
  • Nakei Nairobi

Mbilia Bel's recordings with Tabu Ley form one of the most recognisable chapters of Afrisa's recording history. These songs are especially important when examining the orchestra's popularity during the 1980s and its relationship with East African audiences.

Live Recordings: Zaïre 74

Afrisa's participation in Zaïre 74 provides important live material. Recordings associated with the festival include:

  • Celicia
  • Salongo, Part 1
  • Salongo, Part 2
  • Annie

These performances preserve a different side of the orchestra from its studio recordings and offer an opportunity to study its live arrangements and stage sound.

THE LATER RECORDINGS

The American Period

Muzina

Muzina belongs to the later Afrisa period and was recorded for Rounder Records by the smaller formation that continued working internationally during the 1990s.

Africa Worldwide

Africa Worldwide is another important recording from this later period. It demonstrates that Tabu Ley's Afrisa project continued beyond the classic Kinshasa orchestra years.

EAST AFRICAN CONNECTION

Nakei Nairobi

The title Nakei Nairobi is particularly significant in the history of Afrisa's relationship with Kenya. It reflects the strong connection between Congolese musicians and East African audiences.

The song is closely associated with the Mbilia Bel and Tabu Ley period. Its place in the orchestra's history makes it an important recording to investigate through original releases and historical accounts of Afrisa's Kenyan tours.

DISCOGRAPHICAL CAUTION

Original Releases and Reissues

A complete Afrisa discography requires examination of original record labels, catalogue numbers, record sleeves, matrix information and release credits.

This page is a selected introduction rather than a claim to list every single, album or recording. Some songs were issued in different markets under different compilations or spellings.

Where an original release date or musician credit cannot be established confidently, it is better to leave the detail open than to publish a misleading date.
