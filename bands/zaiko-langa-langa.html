🎸 Zaïko Langa Langa

Founded in Kinshasa in December 1969, Zaïko Langa Langa became a defining force in the development of Congolese popular music. Its story is tied to generations of singers, guitarists, percussionists and performers whose sound influenced audiences far beyond the two Congos.

🕰️ 1968–1969 — The Road to Zaïko Langa Langa

Before the formation of Zaïko Langa Langa, a group known as Bel Guide National was already active from around 1967. The musicians named in the research notes include Nyoka Longo, Manuaka Waku, Enoch Zamuangana, Teddy Sukami and Gege Mangaya. D.V. Moanda served as the group's administrator.

According to the notes, an important encounter took place on 23 December 1969 during a Bel Guide rehearsal. Jules Presley, the stage name used by Jules Shungu Wembadio, approached the rehearsal. He was a friend of the Mangaya cousins.

Manuaka Waku accompanied Jules on guitar while Jules sang. D.V. Moanda was impressed by the performance and decided to dissolve Bel Guide in order to establish a new group, retaining Jules Shungu and some of the existing musicians.

24 December 1969 — The Founding Meeting

The research notes place the founding meeting at approximately 3 p.m. on 24 December 1969, at the Mangaya family home, 10 Avenue Popo Kabaka.

The people named in connection with the formation meeting are:

  • D.V. Moanda
  • Henri Mongombe
  • Marcellin Belo
  • André Bita

The notes identify Jules Shungu, Manuaka Waku, Josart Nyoka Longo and Teddy Sukami among the co-founders. Other musicians joined in the days that followed, including Matima Mpioso, Simeon Mavuela and Evoloko Joker.

The two dates are kept distinct in this archive: 23 December is associated with the rehearsal encounter, while 24 December is associated with the founding meeting.

🎤 1970–1972 — First Concerts and Recordings

The first concert identified in the notes took place on 24 March 1970 at Bar Dancing Hawai.

During 1970, the group recorded under Polydor Records and the Congolese label DILD. Two early singles identified in the notes are:

  • Mosinzo Ngayga — credited to Teddy Sukami.
  • Pauline — credited to Jules Presley Shungu.

Nyoka Longo later released his first song, identified in the notes as La Tout Neige.

The Pop and Typical Sections

The early orchestra was organised into two sections: Pop and Typical. Mashakado Nzolantima is identified in the notes as belonging to the Pop section.

The early personnel lists include singers Jules Shungu Wembadio, Josart Nyoka Longo, Antoine Evoloko Bitumba, Simeon Mavuela, Pierre Nkumu, Mashakado Nzolantima and Bimi Ombabe.

Instrumentalists named include Felix Manuaka Waku, Enoch Zamuangana, Teddy Sukami, Damien Ndebo, Zephyrin Matiana Mpioso, Beadoin Mitshi and Ephraim. The notes identify Matiana Mpioso as coming from AC Juventus de Matonge.

A Different Orchestra Sound

One of the band's early musical distinctions, as described in the notes, was the removal of the horn section. This was presented as a contrast to the arrangement associated with TPOK Jazz.

The notes place Zaïko Langa Langa within a third generation of Congolese popular music, following earlier figures such as Wendo, Adou Elanga and Antoine Kasongo, and the generation represented by TPOK Jazz and African Jazz.

Between 1971 and 1974, the band is recorded in the notes as having published more than 50 singles.

🥁 1971–1974 — New Musicians and the Cavacha Era

In early 1971, several musicians are recorded as joining the group:

  • Muaka Mbeka, known as Bapius — bass
  • Belobi N'Ekerne, known as Mendjo — percussion
  • Efouge Tsekofeta, known as Gina wa Gina — vocals

Bapius and the Bass Style

Bapius is credited in the notes with establishing a bass style called Trombone or Kindobika. The account connects the style to traditional Kongo music and boy scout dances.

Gina wa Gina and Consolation

Efouge Tsekofeta gained prominence following the song Consolation, identified in the notes with 1972.

The Cavacha Story

One account in the research notes traces the development of Cavacha to a 1973 train journey from Pointe-Noire to Kinshasa.

During the journey, the musicians were singing. Mendjo was holding his drumsticks, and Evoloko suggested imitating the sound of the moving train using the snare drum. The account connects this experiment to the Cavacha rhythm, associated with the snare drum and hi-hat.

According to Uncle Bapius, Evoloko's 1973 composition Mbeya Mbeya was among the first songs to feature the Cavacha beat. The rhythm and its associated dance became widely used in Sub-Saharan African music.

Evoloko Joker was also a prominent member of the band and is credited in the notes with the Cavacha dance craze.

Other Changes

The notes record that Belobi, also known as Mendjo, moved from congas to drums after Ombale's departure. Mbuta Matima is also mentioned as joining the group.

Jules Presley later adopted the stage name Papa Wemba.

In 1973, Zaïko Langa Langa received an award identified in the notes as "Best Orchestra of Zaire" from the Association of Music Chroniclers of Zaire.

1974 — Changes and Zaire 74

In mid-1974, Mendjo was arrested and sentenced to 21 months at Ekafula Prison. Bakunde Ilo Pablo, a drummer from Chem Chem Yetu, joined the band. The notes associate him with the compositions Ndouge and Audo.

Bozi Boziana joined as a replacement for Gina Efouge, whose departure is associated in the notes with depression.

In September 1974, Zaïko Langa Langa appeared at the Zaire 74 Music Festival alongside TPOK Jazz, Tabu Ley, Orchestre Stukas, James Brown and other performers.

The band's debut LP is identified as Non Stop Dancing, released in 1974. The notes describe it as containing six songs, including Eluzam and Mbeya Mbeya.

🎼 1974–1979 — Splits, New Groups and International Recognition

December 1974 — The First Major Split

In December 1974, Jules Shungu, Evoloko Joker, Bozi Boziana and Mavuela Somo left Zaïko Langa Langa to establish Isifi Lokole.

The notes trace a succession of related groups:

  • Zaïko Langa Langa
  • Isifi Lokole
  • Yoka Lokole
  • Viva La Musica, formed in December 1976

Following the departures, the notes identify Likinga Redo and Lengi Lenga Nsumbu among the musicians who joined Zaïko. Mbuta Mashakado moved from the Pop section to the Typical section.

1975 — The Ghana Tour

In late 1975, under the production of Henri Bowane, Zaïko Langa Langa toured Ghana for approximately one and a half months.

During the tour, the group recorded its second LP, described in the notes as its first double album: Plaisir De L'Ouest Afrique. The recording took place at Studio Essiebons. The album was later remastered and reissued in 1993 under the title Zaire-Ghana.

1976 — More Departures and Projects

In 1976, Manuaka Waku joined Ray Lema, Bopol Mansiamina and other musicians in forming Les Ya Toupas du Zaire.

Mashakado briefly left for Yoka Lokole. Gina Efonge returned to Zaïko and later left in 1977 to form T.P Libanko.

1977 — FESTAC and Mendjo's Return

Mendjo Belobi was released from prison in 1977. The notes identify this period, from 1977 to 1979, as the band's "Pre Golden Era".

Zaïko participated in FESTAC 77 in Lagos, Nigeria. Manuaka Waku, Nyoka Longo, Likinga Redo and Mashakado Mbuta are identified as having been selected by Tabu Ley to join the National Orchestra of Zaire (ONAZA) for the festival.

Mendjo returned to the band with the song Sangela, sharing drum duties alternately with Pablo.

The notes also associate Bozi Boziana's period with songs including Diana ya Mama, Bibichana and Toutou. Bozi rejoined Zaïko after the failure of Yoka Lokole.

1978–1979 — Further Developments

In 1978, Teddy Sukami created a group called Les Casques Blues, with Lengi Lenga and Yenga Yenga Jr. Sukami's composition Bougo Bouger is recorded in the notes as having been certified gold by SONECA.

Zaïko marked its tenth anniversary in December 1979. An anniversary concert was held at the Palais du Peuple in Kinshasa in early 1980.

🔥 1980–1984 — The Golden Decade and the Rise of Atalaku

The 1980s opened with Manuaku Waku's hit Obi, according to the research notes.

1980 — Zaïko Wawa

In September 1980, disputes within Zaïko led to a mass departure. Manuaka Waku left with Cheikdan Mbuku, Mbuta Sanza, Otis Mbuta and Djudjuchet Luvengoka to form Zaïko Wawa.

Evoloko's Fièvre Mondo is identified in the notes as the Best Song of the Year 1980. Other songs named alongside it are Solomo, Viya and Crois moi.

1981 — Langa Langa Stars and a Period of Inactivity

In September 1981, Bozi Boziana and Evoloko left to form Langa Langa Stars with producer Verckys.

The notes describe a dispute in which Verckys reclaimed equipment at Cine Palladium (Cinemax). Zaïko was then inactive for approximately nine months.

1982 — Nkolo Mboka

In September 1982, the group released its fifth LP and second double album, Nkolo Mboka.

30 October 1982 — The Introduction of Atalaku

The notes identify 30 October 1982 as the date when Atalaku, or musical animators, were officially introduced on the television programme Variété Samedi Soir.

The names recorded in this account are Nono Monzuluku, Bebe Mangituka and percussionist Djerba Mandjeku Makale, who came from Bana Odeon in Kintambo.

1983 — Muvaro and Zekete Zekete

Muvaro was recorded in early 1983 at Studio I.A.D in Brazzaville. The notes identify Beniko Zangilu Popolipo as the performer of the seben.

In August 1983, the group released Zekete Zekete 2eme Episode. The notes describe it as the first album featuring animators and connect it to the Zekete Zekete dance.

1984 — The Death of D.V. Moanda

D.V. Moanda, one of the figures associated with the band's formation, died on 10 January 1984 at the age of 36.

The notes also mention a sponsorship proposal from Gabonese promoter Gustave Bongo and the arrival of Dindo Yogo in 1984.

🎙️ 1988–2011 — Later Splits and the Story of Bimi Ombale

The notes identify Bimi Ombale as having served with Zaïko Langa Langa from 1969 to 1988. His musical development is described as a movement from drumming towards singing and soukous.

1988 — Zaïko Nkolo Mboka and Familia Dei

In 1988, the notes record a split into Zaïko Langa Langa Nkolo Mboka and Zaïko Langa Langa Familia Dei. Bimi Ombale led Familia Dei.

1991–1995 — Basilique Loningisa

Bimi led Familia Dei until 1991, when co-leader Ilo Pablo forced him out, according to the notes.

In 1991, Bimi formed Basilique Loningisa. Karlito Lassa is identified as a guest star. The group split in 1995.

1995–2011 — Frère André

The notes state that Bimi converted to Christianity in 1995 and adopted the name Frère André. He continued singing gospel music until his death in April 2011.

The recorded dates of birth and death are 25 July 1955 and 29 April 2011 respectively.

📝 Historical and Editorial Notes

This archive preserves the spellings, dates and accounts found in the original research notes. The following details require further checking before they can be treated as settled.

  • Bimi Ombabe / Bimi Ombale: Both forms appear in the notes. The early line-up uses Ombabe, while the later account uses Ombale.
  • Matima Mpioso / Mbuta Matima: The notes use both names. Their relationship has not been established here.
  • Cavacha origin account: The train-journey story is preserved as an account attributed to Uncle Bapius and the musicians' recollection.
  • Atalaku introduction: The date and television programme are preserved from the notes and require independent confirmation.
  • Album chronology: The numbering and sequence of Muvaro and Zekete Zekete 2eme Episode should be checked against discographical sources.
  • Song credits and spellings: Song titles and individual credits are retained as recorded in the source notes and should not be treated as fully verified discographical entries.

These editorial flags are part of the archive's commitment to preserving historical material without disguising uncertainty.
