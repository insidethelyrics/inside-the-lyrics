THE AFRICAN JAZZ ARCHIVE

Offshoots and Related Orchestras

Following the musicians, disagreements and musical movements that carried African Jazz's influence into new orchestras.

Introduction: A Musical Legacy Beyond One Orchestra

African Jazz, under the leadership of Joseph Athanase Tshamala Kabasele, better known as Grand Kallé, became an important centre of musical innovation in the Belgian Congo.

Its influence extended beyond its own recordings. Musicians who performed with the orchestra later established or joined other important Congolese and African musical groups.

One of the most consequential moments came in 1963, when disagreements over management, payments and the direction of the orchestra contributed to a major split.

1. African Fiesta

African Fiesta emerged in 1963 following the departure of several leading African Jazz musicians. Among the important figures associated with the breakaway were Tabu Ley Rochereau, Dr Nico and Roger Izeidi.

The new orchestra brought together musicians who had played important roles in African Jazz. It represented a new musical direction rather than a continuation of Grand Kallé's original orchestra.

Important figures

  • Tabu Ley Rochereau – vocalist and one of the leading figures of the breakaway.
  • Dr Nico (Nicolas Kasanda) – influential guitarist.
  • Roger Izeidi – percussionist and an important figure in the orchestra's earlier history.

African Fiesta became a significant force in Congolese popular music. Its subsequent history included further changes in leadership and musical identity.

2. African Fiesta National

African Fiesta National became associated with Tabu Ley Rochereau following the later division within African Fiesta.

Tabu Ley developed a distinctive musical identity through his work as a singer, composer and orchestra leader. His later career made him one of the most influential figures in Congolese rumba and soukous.

African Fiesta National therefore represents one of the important branches of the musical movement that grew out of the 1963 African Jazz split.

3. African Fiesta Sukisa

African Fiesta Sukisa was associated with guitarist Dr Nico after the division of African Fiesta.

Dr Nico was widely admired for his guitar technique and melodic approach. His work helped establish the guitar as a central expressive voice in modern Congolese music.

Through African Fiesta Sukisa, Dr Nico continued developing his musical ideas and leadership beyond his earlier years with African Jazz.

4. Negro Succès

Negro Succès was formed in 1960 by Victor Longomba (Vicky) and André Menga after they departed from African Jazz.

Its formation is an example of how personnel changes and creative disagreements were already producing new orchestras before the major 1963 split.

Negro Succès should therefore be understood as an earlier breakaway connected to African Jazz's changing musical environment, rather than as a direct product of the 1963 African Fiesta split.

5. Vox Africa

Vox Africa became associated with singer Jeannot Bombenga after his departure from the later African Jazz formation.

Bombenga had been part of the orchestra during its post-1963 rebuilding period. His later musical activities demonstrate how African Jazz alumni continued to shape the wider Congolese music scene.

The connection between African Jazz and Vox Africa is part of the broader story of musicians establishing independent identities after leaving established orchestras.

6. African Team de Paris – 1966

In 1966, Grand Kallé worked in Paris with musicians including Manu Dibango and Jean-Serge Essous on a temporary project known as African Team de Paris.

This was a collaborative recording project, not simply another name for the original African Jazz orchestra. Its recordings reflected a wider mixture of Congolese and other African musical influences.

The project is particularly important when tracing Grand Kallé's later musical activities and his continuing collaborations with musicians from different musical backgrounds.

7. African Team – 1970–1971

The later African Team project brought Grand Kallé together with musicians including Manu Dibango, Edouard Lutula, Joseph Mulamba (Mujos), Jean Munsi (Kwamy) and Cuban flutist Don Gonzalo Fernandez.

Its musical approach incorporated Congolese rumba alongside styles such as charanga, mambo, pachanga, merengue and boogaloo.

The project produced recordings including:

  • Ko Ko Ko... Qui Est Là?
  • Suzi Na Edo
  • Africa Boogaloo
  • Safari Muzuri
  • Nzela Mosika

This later African Team should not be confused with the temporary African Team de Paris sessions of 1966.

Understanding the Connections

The following simplified outline shows the main relationships discussed in this archive. It is not intended to represent every musician's career or every historical membership change.

  • African Jazz – led by Grand Kallé.
  • Negro Succès – formed by Vicky Longomba and André Menga in 1960.
  • African Fiesta – emerged from the major 1963 breakaway.
  • African Fiesta National – associated with Tabu Ley Rochereau.
  • African Fiesta Sukisa – associated with Dr Nico.
  • Vox Africa – associated with Jeannot Bombenga's later career.
  • African Team de Paris – a 1966 collaborative project.
  • African Team – a later project around 1970–1971.

Why These Offshoots Matter

The story of African Jazz is also the story of the musicians who passed through it. Their departures, collaborations and independent projects helped spread its musical influence across the Congolese music industry.

African Fiesta, African Fiesta National and African Fiesta Sukisa became important names in their own right. Other projects, including Negro Succès, Vox Africa and African Team, show how interconnected the musical world around Grand Kallé had become.

Studying these orchestras helps us understand African Jazz not only as a band, but as part of a much wider musical network.

Editorial Note

Orchestra histories sometimes differ in their descriptions of membership, formation dates and leadership. This page distinguishes the 1960 Negro Succès formation, the 1963 African Fiesta breakaway, the later African Fiesta divisions and the separate African Team projects.
