Zaïko Langa Langa: Discography & Musical Recordings

Zaïko Langa Langa developed a distinctive musical identity through its recordings, compositions, dance innovations and changing generations of musicians. This archive documents selected songs and releases mentioned in the historical research collected by Inside the Lyrics with Sir Wakho.

The entries are arranged chronologically wherever the available notes provide dates. This is a developing archive, not a claim to be a complete official discography.

🎵 1. Early Recordings: 1970–1972

Zaïko Langa Langa began recording during its early years, working with Polydor Records and the Congolese label DILD. Its early singles helped establish the group's identity.

🥁 2. The Cavacha Era: 1973–1974

The early 1970s saw Zaïko Langa Langa develop a sound and performance identity that would influence popular African dance music. The Cavacha rhythm became an important part of this period.

💿 3. Albums and Major Releases

Non Stop Dancing (1974)

Zaïko Langa Langa released its debut LP, Non Stop Dancing, in 1974. According to the available notes, the album contained six songs, including Mbeya Mbeya and Eluzam.

The other four song titles are not included in the current source notes and are therefore left undocumented here.

Plaisir De L'Ouest Afrique (1975)

In late 1975, the group travelled to Ghana for approximately one and a half months. During this period, it recorded Plaisir De L'Ouest Afrique at Studio Essiebons under the production of Henri Bowane.

The release is described in the notes as the group's second LP and first double album. It was later remastered and reissued in 1993 under the title Zaire-Ghana.

Nkolo Mboka (September 1982)

Released in September 1982, Nkolo Mboka is identified in the notes as Zaïko Langa Langa's fifth LP and second double album.

Muvaro (Early 1983)

Muvaro was recorded in early 1983 at Studio I.A.D in Brazzaville. The recording features a seben played by Beniko Zangilu Popolipo.

Zekete Zekete 2eme Episode (August 1983)

Released in August 1983, this album is described in the notes as the first album featuring animators (Atalaku). Its title is connected to the Zekete Zekete dance.

📝 6. Editorial and Research Notes

  • The six-song tracklist for Non Stop Dancing is incomplete in the available notes. Only Mbeya Mbeya and Eluzam are named.
  • The complete tracklists for Plaisir De L'Ouest Afrique, Nkolo Mboka, Muvaro and Zekete Zekete 2eme Episode are not yet documented here.
  • Song titles such as Mosinzo Ngayga, La Tout Neige, Crois moi and Jbiakina retain the spellings supplied in the source notes.
  • The Bimi Ombale list requires further confirmation of recording dates, album appearances and individual credits.
  • Recognition, certification and release details are reported from the collected notes and require independent confirmation before being treated as definitive discographical records.
