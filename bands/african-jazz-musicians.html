The Musicians of African Jazz

The voices, guitars, horns and rhythms behind Grand Kallé's orchestra.

A personnel directory documenting musicians associated with African Jazz across its different recording periods.

African Jazz was shaped by a remarkable collection of singers and instrumentalists. Some became internationally recognised bandleaders, while others made important contributions as instrumentalists and session performers.

Its personnel changed considerably between the early 1950s and the later recordings associated with Grand Kallé. Musicians also moved between orchestras, recording projects and temporary formations.

This directory groups the documented personnel by their principal instruments and roles. The dates are approximate periods of recorded association, based on the historical personnel research assembled for this archive.

1. Vocalists

African Jazz's vocal identity developed through several generations of singers. Grand Kallé remained its central voice and leader, while other vocalists helped shape its changing sound.

Joseph Athanase Tshamala Kabasele

Known as: Grand Kallé

Role: Lead vocalist, orchestra founder and leader

1950–1970

Grand Kallé was the central figure behind African Jazz. He led the orchestra through its early development, its Brussels performances, the Surboum years and its later transformations.

His musical and organisational influence extended beyond the orchestra through his recording and publishing activities.

Marcelin Laboga

Role: Vocalist

1950–1955

One of the vocalists associated with African Jazz's early recording years.

Charles Mwamba

Known as: Déchaud

Role: Vocalist and guitarist

1950–1963

Déchaud was an important early member of African Jazz. He contributed to its vocal and guitar arrangements and remained associated with the orchestra across several important recording periods.

Philippe Lando

Known as: Rossignol

Role: Vocalist

1957–1958

Rossignol was among the singers associated with the Esengo period. His voice featured alongside Grand Kallé in the recording history of this era.

Roger Izeidi

Role: Vocalist and percussionist

1957–1963

Izeidi was associated with African Jazz as a vocalist and maracas player. He also became important in the orchestra's recording business and the establishment of Surboum African Jazz.

Pascal Tabou

Known as: Tabu Ley Rochereau

Role: Vocalist

1959–1963

Tabu Ley emerged as one of the important young voices associated with African Jazz. His later departure and leadership of African Fiesta marked a major development in Congolese popular music.

Victor Longomba

Known as: Vicky Longomba

Role: Vocalist

1960

Vicky was associated with the special African Jazz formation that travelled to Brussels in 1960. He later became associated with Negro Succès.

Jeannot Bombenga Weyando

Role: Vocalist

1963–1966

Bombenga joined the reorganised African Jazz following the 1963 split. He later became associated with Vox Africa.

Mathieu Kouka

Role: Vocalist

1963–1969

Kouka was among the singers associated with the post-1963 African Jazz. His composition Bébé was later reworked in the recording BB 69.

Jean Nsita

Known as: Bolly / Rolly

Role: Vocalist

1963–1969

Nsita was associated with the reorganised African Jazz during the orchestra's later years.

Freddie Nkounkou

Role: Vocalist

1966

Listed among the vocalists associated with African Jazz in 1966.

Casimir Mbilia

Known as: Casino

Role: Vocalist and percussionist

1966

Casino appears in the personnel records as a vocalist and percussionist during the 1966 period.

Emmanuel Dibango

Known as: Manu Dibango

Role: Vocalist, saxophonist and pianist

1966 and 1970

Manu Dibango's association with Grand Kallé extended beyond singing. He became particularly important through his instrumental contributions and the later African Team recordings.

Alexandre Mayuka

Role: Vocalist

1967–1969

Listed among the vocalists associated with the orchestra's later period.

Joseph Mulamba

Known as: Mujos

Role: Vocalist

1970

Associated with the later African Team recording formation.

Jean Munsi

Known as: Kwamy

Role: Vocalist

1970

Listed among the vocalists associated with the later African Team project.

Research and Personnel Notes

This directory is based on a personnel chronology compiled from Ken Braun's historical account of Grand Kallé and African Jazz.

The dates should be interpreted as approximate periods of documented recording or participation. They do not prove that every musician remained a permanent member throughout the stated period.

Some musicians performed more than one role. For example, Charles Mwamba (Déchaud) appears as both a vocalist and guitarist, while Roger Izeidi appears as a vocalist and percussionist.

Some names also appear in different spellings or forms across discographies. Those variations should be checked against individual recordings and original record labels before being treated as definitive.

The directory is a working historical record and may be expanded as further recording credits and personnel evidence become available.
