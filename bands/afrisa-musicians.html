AFRISA INTERNATIONAL ARCHIVE

Musicians and Performers

The singers, guitarists, instrumentalists and stage performers who helped shape the sound and identity of Afrisa International.

A changing orchestra led by Tabu Ley Rochereau

LEADERSHIP

Tabu Ley Rochereau

Tabu Ley Rochereau was the central personality behind Afrisa International. As a singer, composer, bandleader and organiser, he shaped the orchestra's musical direction and its public identity.

His leadership extended beyond performing. He guided the recruitment of musicians, developed vocal talent, supervised musical arrangements and built recording structures connected with ISA and Genidia.

Afrisa's changing membership reflected his continuing search for new voices and instrumental approaches.

VOCALISTS

The Voices of Afrisa

Sam Mangwana

Sam Mangwana was an important vocalist associated with Tabu Ley's musical circle during the 1970s. His expressive singing contributed to the development of Afrisa's vocal identity. He later established a major independent career and became one of the recognised voices of African popular music.

Mbilia Bel

Mbilia Bel joined Afrisa in 1981 and became one of its most prominent female vocalists. Her voice and stage presence brought a new dimension to the orchestra's performances.

Her association with Tabu Ley produced important recordings including Mpeve Ya Longo, Eswi Yo Wapi and Boya Ye. Her later independent career further established her place in Congolese music history.

Faya Tess

Faya Tess became associated with Afrisa during the later 1980s. She emerged as one of the female vocalists connected with the orchestra's later period and continued her own musical career.

Dodo Munoko

Dodo Munoko is among the singers associated with Afrisa's changing vocal lineup. His name appears in accounts of the orchestra's personnel, although detailed period-by-period credits require further discographical verification.

Mimi Ley

Mimi Ley was associated with Tabu Ley's musical circle and the wider Afrisa story. Her individual recording and performance credits should be considered separately when building a detailed personnel chronology.

Madilu System

Madilu System's musical career intersected with the wider world of Congolese orchestras and Tabu Ley's musical circle. He later became particularly well known for his work with T.P. OK Jazz. His exact Afrisa period and recording credits should be verified before assigning specific songs to him.

GUITARISTS

The Guitar Section

Dino Vangu

Dino Vangu became one of the defining guitarists associated with Afrisa. He joined the orchestra in the 1970s and became particularly important for his solo guitar work. His playing contributed to the orchestra's melodic and instrumental identity.

Lokassa ya Mbongo

Lokassa ya Mbongo was an important rhythm guitarist associated with Afrisa. His guitar work contributed to the interlocking rhythmic patterns that supported the orchestra's arrangements. He later became recognised internationally through his work with other African musicians.

Mavatiku Visi (Michelino)

Mavatiku Visi, popularly known as Michelino, was an important guitarist in the Congolese music scene. His association with Afrisa forms part of the orchestra's evolving instrumental history.

Dizzy Mandjeku

Dizzy Mandjeku is another guitarist associated with Afrisa's musical world. His career extended beyond one orchestra, reflecting the movement of instrumentalists through the Congolese music industry.

Bopol Mansiamina

Bopol Mansiamina was a guitarist and bassist associated with the Congolese music scene and Afrisa's wider personnel history. His later work included collaborations with musicians from different African musical traditions.

OTHER INSTRUMENTALISTS

Beyond the Guitars

Seskain Molenga

Seskain Molenga is associated with Afrisa's percussion and drumming history. His work belongs to the wider tradition of Congolese musicians whose rhythmic contributions supported the orchestral sound.

Ringo Moya

Ringo Moya is another drummer associated with Afrisa's personnel history. Specific recording credits and years of service should be checked against original releases.

Mekanisi Modero

Mekanisi Modero is associated with the orchestra's wider instrumental and compositional history. Individual credits should be matched to the relevant recordings before assigning a precise role or period.

STAGE PRESENTATION

The Rocherettes

The Rocherettes were female dancers and performers associated with Tabu Ley's stage presentation. They formed part of Afrisa's distinctive visual identity, helping transform performances into coordinated musical and choreographic productions.

Their presence reflected the importance Tabu Ley placed on stagecraft, movement and presentation alongside musical performance.

The Rocherettes should be documented as a performance group rather than treated as a conventional instrumental section of the orchestra.

EDITORIAL NOTE

A Changing Lineup

Afrisa International operated across several decades. The names on this page represent different periods and associations, not one simultaneous lineup. Some musicians moved between orchestras, worked as session performers or developed independent careers.

We will continue refining this roster as original record sleeves, release credits, interviews and reliable discographical sources provide stronger evidence of individual roles and dates.
