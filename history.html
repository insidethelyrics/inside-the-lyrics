The history of African music

From community traditions to a global sound

Explore the musicians, movements, and cultural moments that shaped African musical life—and continue to inspire new generations.

The foundations

Music, memory, and community

Across the continent, music has long been woven into community life: accompanying celebrations and ceremonies, carrying stories, marking important events, and passing knowledge between generations. Rhythms, dance, call-and-response, and a wide range of instruments remain essential to the many musical traditions that inform contemporary sounds.

A few chapters in a much bigger story

Sounds through the decades

1940s–1950s · Congo

Modern rumba takes shape

Congolese musicians developed a distinctive urban popular sound, drawing on local traditions and international influences. Early singers and orchestras helped establish a new musical identity.

Listen for: Wendo Kolosoy and Grand Kallé.

1960s · Independence & new voices

Music in a changing era

Political and cultural change brought new energy to music and public life. Orchestras reached wider audiences, and songs became powerful expressions of identity, celebration, and everyday experience.

Explore: Grand Kallé, Tabu Ley, Franco Luambo, and Vicky Longomba.

1970s · Orchestra culture

A golden era of bands

Large ensembles and influential bandleaders developed rich, instantly recognizable sounds. Their musicianship and friendly rivalry helped push Congolese rumba in new directions.

Explore: OK Jazz, Orchestra Vévé, Afrisa International, and Zaïko Langa Langa.

1980s · Soukous travels

Guitars, dance, and a wider audience

More dance-oriented guitar styles helped Congolese music travel across Africa and beyond, carried by touring musicians, recordings, and vibrant live performances.

Explore: Loketo, Soukous Stars, and African All-Stars.

Tanzania · Dansi

Orchestras sing in Swahili

Tanzanian dansi built a lively band tradition around guitars, brass, dance rhythms, and Swahili storytelling. Its artists and orchestras created a sound with a character all its own.

Explore: Mbaraka Mwinshehe, Remmy Ongala, and Marijani Rajab.

Today · A connected world

Tradition meets new technology

Recording, radio, migration, streaming, and social platforms continue to change how music is made and shared. Older traditions remain an active source of inspiration for new artists.

The story continues with every generation of musicians and listeners.
